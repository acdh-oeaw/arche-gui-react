What is ARCHE?

ARCHE (A Resource Centre for the HumanitiEs) is a service that offers stable and persistent hosting as well as the dissemination of digital research data and resources for the Austrian humanities community.

If you have resources, would like to share them, and want to make sure that the data will be around in the future, contact us. We offer both archiving and online availability for your resources.

Das Benediktinerstift Melk wurde um 985 als Kanonikerstift gegründet und 1089 von Benediktinern besiedelt. Die Patrone sind Koloman, Maria, Petrus und Paulus. Der Bestand umfasst 1858 Handschriften, davon rund 1230 mittelalterliche Handschriften (9.–16. Jh.), sowie 798 Inkunabeln in 737 Bänden. Frühdrucke sind nur zum Teil gesondert aufgestellt, sodass keine Zahlenangabe möglich ist. Außerdem sind 1470 Fragmente in Handschriften und Drucken sowie 268 lose Fragmente vorhanden, davon 28 in hebräischer und 5 in deutscher Sprache. Der Bestand an Druckwerken umfasst aus dem 16. Jh. 1470 Titel in 2350 Bänden, etwa 4500 Bände aus dem 17. Jh., rund 16000 Bände aus dem 18. Jh. sowie etwa 31150 Bände aus dem 19. Jh.

Mittelalterliche Handschriften des Benediktinerklosters St. Georgenberg-Fiecht

Die mittelalterlichen Handschriften und Handschriftenfragmente aus der Bibliothek des 1138 gegründeten Benediktinerklosters St. Georgenberg in Stans (Tirol) zählen zu den kostbarsten Tiroler Kulturschätzen. EtMirA - Etruscan Mirrors in Austria

##### Overall Description: Etruscan bronze mirrors represent one of the largest object categories within ancient Etruscan art, dating from the second half of the 6th to the 2nd century BCE . The reverse of these mostly rounded mirrors was often decorated with an engraved figurative scene, usually a mythological depiction or a scene from everyday life. Occasionally, Etruscan inscriptions explain the depicted subject. Iconographically and epigraphically, the mirrors constitute an important and highly informative object group for the study of the cultures of antiquity. The aims of the FWF project "Etruscan Mirrors in Austria" (EtMirA) are the comprehensive scholarly analysis (description, state of preservation, dating, interpretation of the scene and iconographic comparisons, place of production, acquisition history) and contemporary documentation of 59 Etruscan bronze mirrors of various types held in public collections in Austria. A large portion of the collection is housed in the Kunsthistorisches Museum in Vienna, with further pieces in the Joanneum Graz, the Ferdinandeum Innsbruck, the Wels City Museum, and the Höbarth Museum in Horn, Lower Austria. The results will be published as the volume ÖSTERREICH /AUSTRIA in the international publication series Corpus Speculorum Etruscorum (CSE). The project is a collaboration between the Department of Ancient History, Papyrology and Epigraphy of the University of Vienna, the Collection of Collection of Greek and Roman Antiquities of the Kunsthistorisches Museum in Vienna, and the Computer Vision Lab (CVL) at TU Wien, and is led by the Viennese Etruscologist Petra Amann. ##### Technical Description: Within the "Etruscan Mirrors in Austria" project, the archived data represents a multi-modal digital repository of 59 mirrors held in public collections in Austria. The data content is split between global geometry and high-frequency surface detail. Structured Light (SL) scanners capture the overall 3D shape, providing a reference geometry for cross-sections and outlines. Simultaneously, custom-developed Photometric Stereo (PS) captures minute engravings and relief structures by generating normal, albedo, and depth maps which can be leveraged for extracted the figurative motifs present on the mirrors. The extent of the archive is comprehensive, covering all known handle mirrors and complex box mirrors found in public collections in Austria. All data is standardized using a strict naming convention and exported into open formats like .glb and lossless compressed 16/48-bit TIFF for long-term preservation. *The logo is based on the 3D scan for Inv. Nr. `KHM-ANSA VI 3007` and has been modified using Google Gemini.*

Watermarks in the Music Manuscripts of Franz Schubert

### This data curation is still in progress. The claimed dataset will be made available during autumn of 2026. The present data collection encompasses the materials and research data produced during two projects. The majority was created during the project „Digitization, Recognition, and Automated Clustering of Watermarks in the Music Manuscripts of Franz Schubert“ (DRACMarkS), funded through the Heritage Science Austria programme of the Austrian Academy of Sciences (ÖAW) and lead by Günther Koliander and Andrea Lindmayr-Brandl. Additional data (collections A-Wgm and A-Wwgm) were created during an additional project “Die Winterreise” im Infrarot funded by Stadt Wien and lead by Vasiliki Papadopoulou. The projects offer a new perspective on the watermarks in Franz Schubert’s music manuscripts, employing thermography, machine learning, and signal processing to create digital reproductions of watermarks, to categorise them automatically, and to refine the dating of previously undated autographs. Central to the collection are thermograms of all pages of approximately 400 Schubert music manuscripts held in Vienna, recorded using an infrared camera, which render watermarks visible without any overlay from the written content. The image data are linked to XML metadata based on the encoding standard of the Music Encoding Initiative (MEI). In addition, the collection contains the code developed within the project for clustering, image post-processing, and visualisation in the QuickQuadrantCombinator. The data are publicly accessible on the platform schubert-digital.at.

Letters and travel accounts by Alphons Mylius

The collection contains digital facsimiles of a notebook comprising handwritten copies of letters and travel accounts by Alphons Mylius. The transcriptions in German Kurrent script are almost certainly by Mylius’ mother Maria Dumonte, they begin with the heading ‘My dear Alphonse’s journey in Africa’. In 1905 Mylius travelled through Ethiopia and Kafa, accompanied, amongst others, by the African explorer Julius Friedrich Bieber. Bieber documented many aspects of the life, culture and history of the people living in the Kafa region, thereby making a major contribution to laying the foundations for further research into Kafa. One of his major works is the two-volume book: *Kaffa: An Ancient Cushitic People in Central Africa*. Mylius’ original letters are not available; it is therefore impossible to verify whether Maria Dumonte’s copies are accurate and complete.

Nachlass Konrad Bayer - Korrespondenzen und Taschenkalender

Der Wiener Dichter Konrad Bayer (1932-1964), dessen Stammnachlass von der Assoziation zum blauen Laub archivarisch betreut wird, war als Mitglied der “Wiener Gruppe” ein wichtiger Akteur der österreichischen Nachkriegsavantgarde. Im Rahmen des Förderprogramms “Digitale Transformation” des BMWKMS wird in Zusammenarbeit mit dem ACDH ein Teil des Nachlasses öffentlich zugänglich gemacht. Inhalt der Sammlung sind Digitalisate von Bayers Taschenkalendern aus den Jahren 1953-1964, in die er literarische Notizen, Termine und Kontakte eintrug, sowie der Großteil seiner erhaltenen Korrespondenz. Diese umfasst Schriftverkehr mit Verlagen und Zeitschriften (Rowohlt, manuskripte), Briefe und Postkarten von künstlerischen Wegbegleiter:innen (H.C. Artmann, Ferry Radax, Ida Szigethy) sowie private Korrespondenz, darunter der umfangreiche Briefwechsel mit seiner Partnerin Traudl Bayer.
