What is ARCHE?

ARCHE (A Resource Centre for the HumanitiEs) is a service that offers stable and persistent hosting as well as the dissemination of digital research data and resources for the Austrian humanities community.

If you have resources, would like to share them, and want to make sure that the data will be around in the future, contact us. We offer both archiving and online availability for your resources.

Watermarks in the Music Manuscripts of Franz Schubert

This data curation is still in progress. The claimed dataset will be made available during autumn of 2026.

The present data collection encompasses the materials and research data produced during two projects. The majority was created during the project “Digitization, Recognition, and Automated Clustering of Watermarks in the Music Manuscripts of Franz Schubert“ (DRACMarkS), funded through the Heritage Science Austria programme of the Austrian Academy of Sciences (OeAW) and lead by Günther Koliander, Andrea Lindmayr-Brandl and Katharina Loose-Einfalt. Additional data (collections A-Wgm and A-Wwgm) were created during an additional project “Die Winterreise” im Infrarot funded by Stadt Wien and lead by Vasiliki Papadopoulou. The projects offer a new perspective on the watermarks in Franz Schubert’s music manuscripts, employing thermography, machine learning, and signal processing to create digital reproductions of watermarks, to categorise them automatically, and to refine the dating of previously undated autographs. Central to the collection are thermograms of all pages of approximately 400 Schubert music manuscripts held in Vienna, recorded using an infrared camera, which render watermarks visible without any overlay from the written content. The image data are linked to XML metadata based on the encoding standard of the Music Encoding Initiative (MEI). In addition, the collection contains the code developed within the project for clustering, image post-processing, and visualisation in the QuickQuadrantCombinator. The data are publicly accessible on the platform schubert-digital.at.

JB80

Datenbank mit über 30.000 bibliographischen Einträgen, die nach folgenden Kriterien gesammelt wurden:

  • Japanbezug
  • deutschsprachig
  • veröffentlicht zwischen 1980 und 2000

Berücksichtigt wurden sowohl selbständige Publikationen (Monographien, Sammelwerke, akademische Schriften und Ausstellungskataloge sowie Serien und Periodika) als auch unselbständig erschienene Aufsätze in Zeitschriften und Sammelwerken, in der Regel aber keine Zeitungsartikel.

  • Thematischer Bezug: Die Bibliographie hat zuallererst den Anspruch, Texte, die sich wissenschaftlich mit Japan auseinandersetzen, darzustellen. Der Fokus liegt daher auf Werken mit humanwissenschaftlicher Relevanz im weitesten Sinne, etwa kultur-, gesellschafts- und wirtschaftswissenschaftliche Untersuchungen. Es wurden aber auch Übersetzungen japanischer Literatur, literarische Werke mit Japanbezug sowie populärwissenschaftliche Bücher, Ratgeber, Reiseführer und Ähnliches erfasst. Rein naturwissenschaftlich-technische Themen wurden hingegen nicht systematisch aufgenommen.
  • Sprache: Die meisten erfassten Publikationen sind auf Deutsch verfasst, es wurden aber auch nicht-deutschsprachige Beiträge in die Sammlung einbezogen, wenn sie im deutschen Sprachraum entstanden sind.
  • Zeitraum: Publikationen aus den Jahren 1980 bis 1999 wurden systematisch gesucht und aufgenommen. Für die frühen 2000er Jahre gibt es einige Einträge jedoch wurde dieser Zeitraum nur noch kursorisch erfasst. Zur Zeit besteht nicht die Absicht, die Bibliographie weiter zu führen.

Ein Thesaurus, der aus über 500 hierarchisch strukturierten Schlagworten besteht. Die Verschlagwortung wurde dabei extensiv durchgeführt, das heißt, eine Publikation wurde zumeist nicht nur einem, sondern mehreren relevanten Themen zugeordnet.

Addendum to: ‘The path less travelled’? A case study of cross-regional connectivity in the third millennium BC from the Madra River Delta on the coastline of northwest Anatolia

The interdisciplinary Madra River Delta Project was carried out under the direction of Kyriacos Lambrianides and Nigel Spencer from 1994–97, and provided numerous prehistoric finds from surface surveys and exploratory excavations. The prehistoric material culture assemblage from the project has been prepared for publication by Barbara Horejs and Maria Röcklinger as part of a joint publication project with the project directors. The close proximity of the region to the countryside of Pergamon, investigated since 2008 (see the project Survey of the prehistoric surroundings of Pergamon https://www.oeaw.ac.at/en/oeai/research/prehistory-wana-archaeology/prehistoric-phenomena/survey-of-the-prehistoric-surroundings-of-pergamon), allows a direct comparative analysis of micro-regional characteristics that will contribute to a new definition of the region between the so-called cultural horizons of Troy, Yortan and the Bay of İzmir in the Early Bronze Age. Typological frameworks for the finds in the Madra River Delta, contextualised within the broader cultural context, have been developed in our study based on the documentation of the survey and excavations of the Delta sites of Yeldeğirmentepe and Hüyücektepe. In 2016 a Madra River Delta Project workshop with N. Spencer was organized in Vienna. The aim of the meeting was to discuss the final publication of the project, which is being published in the Annual of the British School at Athens volume 116, 2021. We would like to express our sincere thanks to a generous grant from The Institute for Aegean Prehistory (INSTAP) who supported our publication preparation work and the development of this online archive.

DIAUMA - Digitizing Austrian Dialect Maps

The aim of this project was the digitisation of historical language and dialect maps of the (Central) European area from 1924-1956 and the collection of historical information on these maps in a database. The historical data form the basis for a critical examination of the maps, i.e. for a historical contextualisation. Thus, the database contains information on the authors or draughtsmen of the maps, the institutional background of their creation, the year of origin of the maps, the publisher who published them. Both the scans of the maps and the data mentioned are thus accessible in the form of a digital visualisation. The Project Austrian Dialect Cartography 1924-1956. Digitisation, contextualisation, visualisation is a cooperative project of the "Wörterbuch der bairischen Mundndarten in Österreich" (WBÖ) of the department "Variation und Wandel des Deutschen in Österreich" (VaWADiÖ) of the Austrian Centre for Digital Humanities (ACDH) at the Austrian Academy of Sciences and a satellite project of the special research area "Deutsch in Österreich" (DiÖ) of the Universities of Vienna, Graz and Salzburg and the Austrian Academy of Sciences.

Ladder: a corpus for pragmatic competences in Italian L1/L2

A collection of elicited e-mails and instant messages (written and oral) for the pragmatic analysis of requests, cancellations, apologies and other speech acts collected within the LADDER project (2019-31.3.2024). Data was collected between April 2020 and December 2023 using a discourse completion task (DCT). The data comprises of e-mails, instant messages, and oral messages. The informants include German learners of Italian between A2-C1 level according to the CEFR, most of whom are students living in Tyrol (Austria), native speakers of Italian, most of whom are students from Rome (Italy), and non-native speakers of Italian accessing the Exam CertIt for the B1 level in Italian. The data of Austrian learners were largely collected by students of the undergraduate seminar Insegnare la pragmatica at the Institute of Didactics of the University of Innsbruck, under the guidance of Dr. Nicola Brocca. The data of native speakers were largely collected from students of foreign languages at the University RomaTre, in collaboration with Prof. Elena Nuzzo. The DCTs have been conducted with online questionnaires. Along with the texts, metadata were also registered with the help of an online questionnaire giving sociolinguistic information about the informant (age, self-assessed language level, place of residence, native language, etc.). The DCTs aim to elicit linguistic acts of request and refusal in increasing levels of social distance and different media (Taguchi & Roever, 2017, pp. 85, 231). The DCTs elicit different speech acts (requests and refusals) with different degrees of formality (study/work or free time), directed at different people (lecturer, friend, boss) and in different media (mail or instant messaging). The scenarios represent authentic circumstances for the students. The instrument is descripted in the READ_ME text files.

45 Manuscripts from the Dominican Convent Vienna

The collection contains digital copies and metadata of 45 exemplary manuscripts from the Dominican convent in Vienna. As is seldom realized by the interested public, monastery libraries in Austria harbour a rich cultural manuscript heritage, which they have guarded and preserved for centuries. Therefore, they open up a wide range of perspectives for all kinds of cultural studies, be it library history, book illumination, music, liturgical, intellectual and social history, indeed for all fields of historical research. The Dominican monastery in Vienna was founded in 1225 by Duke Leopold VI of Babenberg. The newly established convent was one of the oldest in the German-speaking world and was soon to develop into an important spiritual centre. The foundation was accompanied by the establishment of a "house study", which in the late Middle Ages was characterized by close ties to the University of Vienna. Today, the continuously growing library still reflects the intellectual environment of this scholarly centre as well as the rich exchange and permanent contact between the Dominicans and other religious communities. All in all, the present collection offers an insight into the exceptionally broad cultural heritage of the Viennese Order of Preachers.

Choral Manuscripts of the Central Library of the Franciscan Province of Graz

The Central Library of the Franciscan Monastery in Graz holds numerous chant sources from the beginning of the 14th century to the 19th century. These manuscripts and prints have received little research attention. Just under a third of the 56 sources date from the 14th, 15th and 16th centuries, two thirds from the 17th and 18th centuries, and thus form a strong, representative collection for the study of the Baroque chorale, which is little known today. The relationship between the Italian (printed) tradition and the Austrian manuscript tradition in the musical versions of antiphons and other chants for Franciscan feasts from the late Middle Ages to the 18th century should also be noted. The existing local choral tradition of the Franciscan Order is still largely terra incognita, despite a good source situation. While the history of Austrian music in the imperial and monastic centres of the monastic orders is relatively well known, especially with regard to the figural music of the 18th century, much remains in the dark with regard to the liturgical-musical practice of the widespread mendicants and their clientele. The cataloguing and online publication of this corpus of sources can at least partially fill this gap and enable further research. The project was funded by the European Union's NextGenerationEU fund and the Austrian Federal Ministry of Art, Culture, Public Service and Sport.

Family Matters: The Correspondence of Empress Eleonora Magdalena of Pfalz-Neuburg

The collection contains the digital copies and metadata for approx. 1,600 letters from the epistolary correspondence of the Habsburg Empress Eleonora Magdalena of Palatinate-Neuburg (1655–1720). These are 1,151 handwritten letters from the empress to her father Philipp Wilhelm of Palatinate-Neuburg (1615–1690) and her brother Johann Wilhelm of Palatinate-Neuburg (1658–1716). In addition, 56 letter drafts or copies of letters from her father and 381 drafts or copies of letters from her brother to the empress are recorded. The collected material thus documents only a fraction of the much more extensive epistolary correspondence of the empress. With over 3,300 pages, it nevertheless represents the most comprehensive collection of letters from a Habsburg woman dated before Maria Theresa. The letters preserved today in the Bavarian State Archives in Munich show a varied array of content; their topics range from pregnancy and health to music and hunting at court to diplomatic negotiations and the wars involving the Holy Roman Empire and Europe in general in the decades around the turn of the 18th century. Most importantly the empress's letters clearly reveal her agency in relation to dynasty, rule and European politics and show her to be an informed and strategic female political actor.

IUENNA - openIng the soUthErn jauNtal as a micro-regioN for future Archaeology

The IUENNA (openIng the soUthErn jauNtal as a micro-regioN for future Archaeology) collection is an comprehensive set of digital archaeological data that enhances the use of digital methods in Austrian archaeology. It addresses complex cultural-historical questions, shapes Digital Humanities in Classics, and secures digital cultural knowledge for the long term. Funded by the Go!Digital 3.0 program of the Austrian Academy of Sciences, the IUENNA collection focuses on the archaeological micro-region of Jauntal/Podjuna (Carinthia, Austria). The project involved the State Museum for Carinthia (kärnten.museum), the Austrian Archaeological Institute (ÖAI) at the Austrian Academy of Sciences (ÖAW), the Austrian Centre for Digital Humanities and Cultural Heritage (ACDH-CH) at the ÖAW, the Federal Monuments Authority (BDA), and ARDIG – Archäologischer Dienst GesmbH. IUENNA represents an open science approach, adhering to the FAIR (Findability, Accessibility, Interoperability, and Reusability) and CARE (Collective benefit, Authority to control, Responsibility, and Ethics) principles. The IUENNA collection aims to provide an exemplary model study and a sustainable long-term archive of archaeological data from one of the most critical Late Antique landscapes in the Southeast Alpine region. The collection features a newly designed, semantically defined folder structure that organizes data into six sub-collections which are primarily categorized by spatial and organizational criteria on the top level:

  • Hemmaberg (HB) – Data on the Late Antique settlement at Hemmaberg; includes born-digital data.
  • Globasnitz (GLO) – Data on the rural settlement of Iuenna at the foot of Hemmaberg, in the current municipality of Globasnitz; includes born-digital data.
  • Jaunstein (JAU) – Data on a burial site in Jaunstein near Iuenna, in the current municipality of Globasnitz; includes born-digital data.
  • Sankt Stefan (STE) – Data on a villa in Sankt Stefan near Iuenna, in the current municipality of Globasnitz; includes born-digital data.
  • Jauntal (TAL) – Archaeological data on the project area; includes born-digital data.
  • Retrodigitalisate (RET) – Retrodigitized analog documentation from the kärnten.museum, primarily concerning Hemmaberg but also other sites.

Subcollections with born-digital data are then divided according to their various data types in corresponding folders (Sub-Sub-Collections Level 1), such as vector and raster data, databases, texts and tables, as well as results and plans. These are further subdivided into more specific categories (Sub-Sub-Collections Level 2 etc.) like geophysics, photos, drawings, bioarchaeology, and protocols. On the contrary, the dataset of retrodigitized documents mirrors the analog archive structure, organizing data into folders and subfolders.

Consequently, the IUENNA collection is a diverse array of digital and retrodigitized resources focused on archaeological research and documentation in Jauntal. It includes data from archaeology, geophysics, born-digital data, documentary photography, environmental archaeology, restoration, and architectural research. The data has been collected over decades, making it highly valuable for future archaeologists. It consists of text, tables, databases, raster datasets, vector datasets, and 3D data. Raster and vector data, for example, are particularly relevant to photography, graphics, and geoinformation fields, representing topographic surfaces, spatial geometries, and including qualitative and quantitative attributes. For long-term archiving, all data was transferred into suitable formats, such as PDF/A for documents and JPG/PNG/TIFF for images, depending on their characteristics. Proprietary file formats, often resulting from specialized software, were converted into well-documented, widely used, and standardized formats for long-term storage. When conversion was not possible, such as with certain (archaeological) databases, they were archived using the BagIt file format.

The learned correspondence of Bernhard and Hieronymus Pez

In 1709, Bernhard Pez, a young Austrian Benedictine monk wrote his first encyclical letters to fellow monasteries in all parts of Europe. His goal was to create a universal bio-bibliographical dictionary of his order, a project that, although it could not be realized over the following decades, inspired many spin-off publications and one of the most interesting scholarly correspondences in eighteenth-century Central Europe. In their exchange with fellow monks and scholars, Bernhard and his brother Hieronymus Pez discussed with their correspondents library and archive holdings of a medieval history oscillating between its ecclesiastical, regional and dynastic dimensions. Of particular interest are sources that are lost today or that regard medieval controversies – such as those between secular and regular clergy, or between pope and emperor – which were still being politicized in the eighteenth century. Two volumes of the Pez letters, covering the years 1709-1718 and roughly half of the entire material, were published in print and PDF in 2010 and 2015. Between 2019 and 2024, financed by FWF project “dQIÖG” (https://doi.org/10.55776/PUD23), these volumes have been transformed into a digital scholarly edition which is available online on the Brill Scholarly Editions Platform since 2024.

Prosopography of the Viennese Merchants (1725–1758): The First Viennese Mercantile Record

The collection contains the digital copies, the transcription and metadata of the first Vienna Mercantile Record. This source, the forerunner of the commercial register, provides the first (more or less) complete list of merchants and their trading companies based in Vienna for the period from 1725 to 1758 without the subjects of the Ottoman Empire. It therefore forms the basis for any prosopographical research into Viennese merchants. Due to partially preserved information on business holdings, equity capitalisation and marriage contracts, the mercantile record offers far more than a mere list of Viennese commercial enterprises. The register is organised alphabetically, the Jewish merchants are listed at the end. An index forms the conclusion. As the first volume of the Viennese Mercantile Record is not a fair copy, but a later compilation characterised by numerous abbreviations, deletions, insertions and cross-references, the text is sometimes difficult to read. The transcription produced is therefore not a scholarly edition, but a suggested reading enriched with metadata. The original record is preserved in the Vienna City and Provincial Archives (2.3.2.B6.1 – Protokoll I, 1725–1758).

Tillich Briefe — Die Korrespondenz von Paul Tillich

The digital edition Paul Tillich – Correspondence 1886–1933 makes accessible, for the first time in scholarly form, a selection of letters from the early life of the German Protestant theologian Paul Tillich (1886–1965). Covering his formative years, the collection documents his personal development, theological formation, and intellectual positioning in the German academic and cultural landscape prior to his emigration. The letters provide insights into Tillich’s education, his service in World War I, his engagement with religious socialism, and his academic rise during the Weimar Republic. They reveal the emergence of Tillich’s theological and philosophical thinking, his social and professional networks, and his increasing political awareness leading up to the National Socialist seizure of power. The edition offers annotated texts, contextual metadata, and is fully aligned with digital edition standards. It contributes to research on early 20th-century theology, German intellectual history, and the political entanglements of religious thought before 1933.

Digital Archive of the International Gustav Mahler Society

The digital collection offers access to the central archive holdings of the International Gustav Mahler Society. Founded in Vienna in 1955, the Society is the oldest and most important institution of international Mahler research. The holdings include numerous printed sources with original entries by Mahler and annotations by editors and performers, event programs, articles and reviews, photos and other documents. The holdings are not only relevant for scholars in the fields of music, politics, history and culture, but also for a wider public interested in the history of Austria, the Habsburg Monarchy, Viennese Modernism and the intertwining of music and politics from the late 19th century to the present day. The collection is also of great interest to active musicians, as conducting scores and orchestral parts with handwritten entries are also part of the collection.

The Account Books of the Upper Chamber Office of the City of Vienna

The Account Books of the Upper Chamber Office of the City of Vienna form a central collection of sources for the history of Vienna in the Middle Ages and the Early Modern period. They cover the period from 1424 to 1768 and contain information on numerous aspects of urban life. The digitisation and in-depth indexing of all 289 volumes was carried out between 2024 and 2026 at the Vienna City and State Archives and at the Austrian Centre for Digital Humanities as part of the project “Digital indexing of the Upper Chamber Office accounts of the City of Vienna,” funded by the Federal Ministry for Housing, Arts, Culture, Media and Sport under the funding programme “Cultural Heritage Digital” and financed by the EU.

The accounting books record – in some cases in aggregated form – all revenues and expenditures of the City of Vienna, thereby providing an overview of its economic situation. However, the relevance of the collection goes beyond this, as the entries also offer insights into the life and political history of the city, its administration, buildings, infrastructure, economy, and population – all over a period of almost 350 years. Since Vienna was at that time an important political and economic centre of supra-regional significance, the accounting books are also relevant for internationally comparative urban historical research. The digitisation and detailed indexing of the accounting books of the Upper Chamber Office of the City of Vienna therefore represents a major enhancement compared to the previously only analogue use of this source collection.

DBO@TEI

This is the TEI version of the database of Bavarian dialects in Austria (WBÖ citation database), which represents the digitized source data of the Dictionary of Bavarian Dialects in Austria (WBÖ). It was originally transcribed in TUSTEP and converted into TEI and serves as one of the building blocks of the “LIÖ – Lexicographical Information System Austria”. The WBÖ citation database is a digitized version of a large portion of the linguistic attestations that have been collected on index cards since the early 20th century as the basis for the Dictionary of Bavarian Dialects in Austria (WBÖ). The aim of the dictionary was and is the lexicographic documentation of the Bavarian dialects of Austria, South Tyrol, and other areas that formerly belonged to historical Austria (during the time of the Habsburg Monarchy), including Bohemia, Moravia, and various language islands in northern Italy. The citation database comprises full-text digitizations of the analogue card catalogue from the letter D onwards and covers, with around 2.4 million entries, approximately 85% of the entire corpus. The collection consists for the most part (about 54%) of material gathered on the basis of 109 “large questionnaires” and nine “supplementary questionnaires” conducted between 1913 and 1937. The main part of the data was collected with the help of volunteer collectors—i.e., dialect-competent volunteers from across the entire survey area. These questionnaire-based collections were supplemented by surveys conducted by trained dialectologists in the form of field research trips (the so-called “Kundfahrten” from 1927–1965) and questionnaire surveys (until 1990). In addition to the questionnaire data, excerpts were compiled from dialectological specialist literature (e.g., dissertations, monographs, and regional dictionaries) and other written sources (such as dialect collections and historical texts, as well as dialect literature, prose, and poetry), accounting for approximately 46% of the material. The collection includes the original TUSTEP data as well. The zip "tustep.zip" contains ~16.000 Tustpe files, ordered in directories matching the drawers holding the matching paper slips.

The Medieval Manuscripts in the Collection of the Gurk Diocesan Archives

The Diocese of Gurk Archives is the historical archive of the Catholic Church in Carinthia. It not only preserves, organizes, and catalogs the records of the diocesan administration dating back to the Middle Ages, but also safeguards the historical collections of over 330 parish archives, including all baptismal, marriage, and death registers from the earliest times up to 1939. In addition to a collection of documents (more than 7,000 items dating from 1177 onward), the archive’s repositories in Klagenfurt also house the holdings of the Gurk Cathedral Chapter Archive as well as the mensal library of the Bishops of Gurk, containing well over 7,000 book titles from the Middle Ages through the late 18th century. The starting point for this project is 254 medieval manuscripts from the episcopal mensal library and the former provostry of Maria Saal, which are preserved in the Gurk Diocese Archives in Klagenfurt. The manuscripts from the prince-bishops’ mensal library and the former Maria Saal provostry—one of the few collections of medieval manuscripts preserved in southern Austria in such quantity and quality—offer insights into the literary and scholarly needs of their former owners and users. The collection spans the period from the 11th to the 18th century. In terms of content, the manuscripts cover all scholarly fields, such as theology, astronomy, physics, law, and zoology. Numerous manuscripts are adorned with elaborate illustrations, ranging from fleuronnée initials and pictorial scenes within the initials to marginal illustrations.

EtMirA - Etruscan Mirrors in Austria

Overall Description:

Etruscan bronze mirrors represent one of the largest object categories within ancient Etruscan art, dating from the second half of the 6th to the 2nd century BCE . The reverse of these mostly rounded mirrors was often decorated with an engraved figurative scene, usually a mythological depiction or a scene from everyday life. Occasionally, Etruscan inscriptions explain the depicted subject. Iconographically and epigraphically, the mirrors constitute an important and highly informative object group for the study of the cultures of antiquity. The aims of the FWF project "Etruscan Mirrors in Austria" (EtMirA) are the comprehensive scholarly analysis (description, state of preservation, dating, interpretation of the scene and iconographic comparisons, place of production, acquisition history) and contemporary documentation of 59 Etruscan bronze mirrors of various types held in public collections in Austria. A large portion of the collection is housed in the Kunsthistorisches Museum in Vienna, with further pieces in the Joanneum Graz, the Ferdinandeum Innsbruck, the Wels City Museum, and the Höbarth Museum in Horn, Lower Austria. The results will be published as the volume ÖSTERREICH /AUSTRIA in the international publication series Corpus Speculorum Etruscorum (CSE). The project is a collaboration between the Department of Ancient History, Papyrology and Epigraphy of the University of Vienna, the Collection of Collection of Greek and Roman Antiquities of the Kunsthistorisches Museum in Vienna, and the Computer Vision Lab (CVL) at TU Wien, and is led by the Viennese Etruscologist Petra Amann.

Technical Description:

Within the "Etruscan Mirrors in Austria" project, the archived data represents a multi-modal digital repository of 59 mirrors held in public collections in Austria. The data content is split between global geometry and high-frequency surface detail. Structured Light (SL) scanners capture the overall 3D shape, providing a reference geometry for cross-sections and outlines. Simultaneously, custom-developed Photometric Stereo (PS) captures minute engravings and relief structures by generating normal, albedo, and depth maps which can be leveraged for extracted the figurative motifs present on the mirrors. The extent of the archive is comprehensive, covering all known handle mirrors and complex box mirrors found in public collections in Austria. All data is standardized using a strict naming convention and exported into open formats like .glb and lossless compressed 16/48-bit TIFF for long-term preservation. The logo is based on the 3D scan for Inv. Nr. KHM-ANSA VI 3007 and has been modified using Google Gemini.

Watermarks in the Music Manuscripts of Franz Schubert

This data curation is still in progress. The claimed dataset will be made available during autumn of 2026.

The present data collection encompasses the materials and research data produced during two projects. The majority was created during the project “Digitization, Recognition, and Automated Clustering of Watermarks in the Music Manuscripts of Franz Schubert“ (DRACMarkS), funded through the Heritage Science Austria programme of the Austrian Academy of Sciences (OeAW) and lead by Günther Koliander, Andrea Lindmayr-Brandl and Katharina Loose-Einfalt. Additional data (collections A-Wgm and A-Wwgm) were created during an additional project “Die Winterreise” im Infrarot funded by Stadt Wien and lead by Vasiliki Papadopoulou. The projects offer a new perspective on the watermarks in Franz Schubert’s music manuscripts, employing thermography, machine learning, and signal processing to create digital reproductions of watermarks, to categorise them automatically, and to refine the dating of previously undated autographs. Central to the collection are thermograms of all pages of approximately 400 Schubert music manuscripts held in Vienna, recorded using an infrared camera, which render watermarks visible without any overlay from the written content. The image data are linked to XML metadata based on the encoding standard of the Music Encoding Initiative (MEI). In addition, the collection contains the code developed within the project for clustering, image post-processing, and visualisation in the QuickQuadrantCombinator. The data are publicly accessible on the platform schubert-digital.at.

Letters and travel accounts by Alphons Mylius

The collection contains digital facsimiles of a notebook comprising handwritten copies of letters and travel accounts by Alphons Mylius. The transcriptions in German Kurrent script are almost certainly by Mylius’ mother Maria Dumonte, they begin with the heading ‘My dear Alphonse’s journey in Africa’. In 1905 Mylius travelled through Ethiopia and Kafa, accompanied, amongst others, by the African explorer Julius Friedrich Bieber. Bieber documented many aspects of the life, culture and history of the people living in the Kafa region, thereby making a major contribution to laying the foundations for further research into Kafa. One of his major works is the two-volume book: Kaffa: An Ancient Cushitic People in Central Africa. Mylius’ original letters are not available; it is therefore impossible to verify whether Maria Dumonte’s copies are accurate and complete.
